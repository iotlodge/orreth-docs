## The problem is entropy

Put twenty agents on one problem and nobody can say who did what, under which rule, for how
much, or how to stop it. Every agent brings its own identity story, its own memory, its own way
of calling a tool, its own idea of what it may do and what it costs. Frameworks add loops; they
do not remove the entropy.

An operating system removed that entropy for programs by taking the rigid parts away from them —
processes, files, permissions, the clock — and holding them in one place under one law. Orreth
does that for agents.

## Because the control is rigid, the experience is fluid

There is **one chat** to every agent in the world, and **THE PANEL**: one live drawing of the world
where the geometry is what is declared, the light is what is happening, and the colour is who did
it — amber a person, cyan an agent, violet the kernel, red a watch or a hold.

People sit outside the machine and apply control: policy, security, a hand on the interlock, the
forensic turn. Orreth applies its ontology to the running state so the world stays
**reproducible, trusted, understood, and evolving**. Intentions such as cost, recovery time and
return on investment are how people control scale.

## Three ways to work

- **Do it yourself.** Build or bring your own agent and seat it here under the same laws.
- **Work with the crew.** You and the residents and firmware craft the words, the skills and the
  plan together, with MITL — the mind-in-the-loop that has read the architecture — at your elbow.
- **Ask the kernel to take the lead.** Say what you want kept true, and the rails bring in the
  planner, the critic and the crew to observe, plan, act and report.

In every one of the three, a consequential act holds until a person says yes. The dial is how
much of the engineering you do — never whether a person is in the loop.

## Where it stands

Kernel **0.1.0** is the first release of Orreth's second architecture. It runs on one laptop today:
two kernels under one law (the Rust kernel `orrethd` and its Python reference), a crew of ten
bodies, the ground and the rails, the gateway, cells, the seat and the desk, and the remediation
rail. The source is public at [github.com/iotlodge/orreth](https://github.com/iotlodge/orreth); the
image is `ghcr.io/iotlodge/orrethd:0.1.0`. The first architecture — seventy-three design dives and
the console they built — is kept whole at the tag `main-v0.72-old-world`, and nothing of it is taught
here.
