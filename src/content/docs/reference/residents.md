---
title: The residents
description: The machine's built-in staff — who they are, what each one may and may not do, and how to tell a resident from a capability specialist.
---

Orreth's built-in staff are **residents**: system agents, one per duty,
shipped as part of the machine (its *firmware* — changing one issues a new
release of the whole machine). Each carries its own unforgeable identity,
thinks through the same metered gateway as everyone else, and appears in the
console by name — click one to talk to it. A resident answers questions
inside its own duty and refers you *by name* to the right colleague when
you've asked the wrong one.

The deepest rule they all live under: **residents deliver governance, never
outcomes.** They keep the record, guard the gates, grade the work, and
answer for their domains — the *purposes* (analysis, research, your
workflows) belong to capabilities and workforce agents built on top.

## The staff you'll meet

| Resident | Duty — and the line it will not cross |
|---|---|
| **becky** | Identity and admission. She alone mints the leases that let an agent in, each chained to the world's pinned root of trust — and every admission waits at a human's gate. She never widens a scope beyond what was asked. |
| **the librarian** | Retrieval. Answers questions from the world's own records, with citations naming each source's floor and fidelity. She fetches; she never invents — a question the record can't ground gets an honest "no memories under this focus." |
| **charlotte** | The tool registry (*the Farm*). Onboards outside tools, pins each to the fingerprint a human approved, watches for changes (a changed tool is quarantined, not trusted), and tests tools beside you in her playground. She answers for her shed — and confesses its edges. |
| **ada** | The model registry (*the Stable*). Keeps the market of available minds — capabilities, prices, provenance — as intelligence, never authority; a mind serves only on a human-approved stall with its terms pinned. |
| **vera** | Evaluation (*the Observatory*'s examiner). Grades finished work under a declared budget ceiling, always signing as herself — the law is structural: no mind ever grades its own work, and her verdicts stage as cards, never levers. |
| **allen** | Infrastructure (*the estate*). The cloud architect: grows container bodies for tools as attested deeds, keeps his own study of the platform's news, and answers infrastructure questions from his own floor. |
| **grace** | Improvement (*the smith*). Reads the evidence of what worked and what wounded, and forges *proposals* — new versions of prompts, rules, skills. Nothing she makes is adopted without a human's signature. |
| **the studio** | Comprehension. Reads every incoming objective, types out what it understood (domains, needs, gaps, confidence), and drafts the plan that waits for your approval. Its reading rides on the card you decide. |
| **quinn** | The stranger. A user-acceptance persona who walks the console as a first-timer on a schedule and files everything that confuses her as signed feedback — the machine's standing humility check. |

## The unembodied keepers

Some duties run without a face — you meet their work, not their voice:

- **vigil**, the warden — content-blind checks at the tool onboarding gate
  (credential patterns, dead probes, manifest drift). Firmware: nobody can
  edit vigil into not looking, though the *words* of his confessions are
  tunable craft.
- **the steward** — the memory metabolism: compresses old records with
  measured loss, keeps touched records warm, on every floor's own schedule.
- **the watchdog** — compares the machine's declared version against what
  is actually running; drift becomes a public accusation that waits for a
  human, never a silent heal.
- **the witness** — the kernel-side dead-man's switch: if the agentic layer
  falls silent past the threshold, the silence is recorded — and, under a
  consent you granted once, rung to your inbox (*the bell*).

## Residents vs. capability specialists

**charles, chad, and charlene** — the trading-desk analysts on the demo —
are *not* residents. They are **capability specialists**: minds that arrive
with an installed capability, live on its floor, and leave with it. The
razor that separates the two layers: *if you can change it in a running
world, it's purpose; if changing it requires a release of the machine, it's
firmware.* Residents are firmware. Everything you build is purpose.
