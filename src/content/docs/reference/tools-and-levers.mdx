Two catalogues are data, not code, in kernel 0.1.0 — files both kernels read, and the tables
below are generated from copies of those files (`spine/tools.v0.json` and `spine/levers.v0.json`
at 0.1.0), so they cannot drift from what the kernel enforces.

## The built-in tools

A **tool** is what a body runs. Its declaration — name, words, input schema, consequence class,
flags — is the kernel's; its execution stays body-side, bound by name, and a body refuses to
start when a declaration has no executor. The kernel authorizes, journals and meters every call
and never runs one itself. A tool not declared in a body's template is refused with a teaching.
MCP-born tools never live here — their server lists them onto the shelf.

The consequence class decides the ladder: **routine** runs at once; **consequential** holds at the
interlock for a person's yes (proof L2); **grave** asks for the person's authenticator code and a
master's click (L3). A tool marked `ground` runs on the ground's own side; one marked `master`
needs a master's seat.

## The levers

A **lever** is what the kernel does with its own hands, through the same door a person would. When
a watch goes red the planner is shown the levers this door serves for that watch's metric and
answers in the catalogue; a routine lever runs at once under the intention's authority, a
consequential one holds at the interlock for a person's click, and a grave one is never pulled by
the kernel. `for` names the watch metrics a lever can remedy (`*` any; none means declared, never
offered as a remedy); `settles` is how long the kernel waits after pulling before it re-reads the
watch.

Every lever pulled lands as `orreth.lever.pulled.v1` on the record, attributed to the intention
that pulled it or the person who did; the panel lights the monitor and the intent track toward the
body it acts on — red while it is held at the interlock for your word.
