Six pictures explain most of Orreth. Each one below starts with the plain
version, then shows the diagram, then names the law it draws. (These were
distilled from an outside architect's deep read of the repository — every
diagram describes machinery that exists today, and the
[what-works-today register](/learn/what-works-today/) stays the honest
boundary.)

## 1 · The shape of the world

A running deployment is a tree of worlds: one **universe** at the top, any
number of **ecosystems** (bounded worlds) inside it, any number of
**fields** (workrooms) inside those, and living identities — agents,
tools, models — at the leaves. Rules only travel down, and may only get
stricter. Memory travels up, and gets summarized on the way. A human sits
above it all, and anything with consequences waits for that human's word.

| Direction | What moves | The law |
|---|---|---|
| **Down** | Rules, policy floors, charters, skills (the **Canon**) | A child world may tighten inherited rules; it may never loosen them. |
| **Up** | Memory, evidence, run statistics (the **Chronicle**) | Raw detail stays near the lives that made it; summarized meaning rises. A parent never reaches in. |
| **Up on demand** | Retrieval | Search locally first, then escalate by time horizon and budget. A partial answer must say what was not searched. |
| **Narrower** | Authority | Delegated permission only ever shrinks — in scope, time, action, and budget. |
| **Back to the human** | Consequence | Agents may detect, plan, propose, and argue. Consequential changes wait at gates; silence never approves. |

## 2 · The atom: a signed record

Everything that happens becomes one kind of thing: a record, named by the
hash of its own bytes and signed by the identity that made it, appended to
one log. Every search index, every dashboard, every view in the console is
a **projection** of that log — disposable, rebuildable, never a second
truth. When something must be destroyed, the governed purge erases the
content everywhere at once and leaves a signed stub proving the
destruction happened.

`}</pre>

**The law it draws:** the signed log is truth; every index and every glass
is a rebuildable projection. Seven retrieval strategies can compete
without creating seven realities, and the worldline cannot be silently
rewritten.

## 3 · How work happens

Work in Orreth is not a chain of function calls — it is a **purpose
graph** that outlives every process involved in it. A human states an
**Objective** (the why). A planning seat breaks it into bounded
**Intentions** (the what), each delegated with only a sliver of context:
the intent, a budget, references, and pinned behavior — never the whole
plan. Workers produce **Observations**; models produce **Thoughts**; and
an independent scribe — never the actor itself — writes the graded record
of what happened.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
sequenceDiagram
    actor Human
    participant O as Orchestration seat
    participant G as Human gate
    participant F as Fingertip at target floor
    participant M as Governed mind / skill
    participant S as Independent scribe / reviewer
    participant C as Chronicle

    Human->>O: OBJECTIVE — the why
    O->>C: signed Objective + proposed plan
    O->>G: readable plan, graph, budget, questions
    G-->>O: approve / decline

    alt approved
        O->>C: signed Intention + Aperture
        Note over O,F: Only the sliver rides down:<br/>intent · budget · refs · behavior pins
        O->>F: INTENTION — delegated what
        F->>M: OBSERVATION task
        M-->>F: THOUGHT / deterministic skill result
        S->>C: RunRecord + grade<br/>author is never the actor
        F-->>O: outcome + evidence
        O->>C: assembled Objective outcome
        O-->>Human: sourced report + dark/waiting branches
    else declined or silent
        O->>C: the refusal / expiry is retained
    end
`}</pre>

The **aperture** — the signed, content-addressed opening a thought ran
inside — makes "what could this mind see?" a lookup instead of an
investigation. And a seat that only knows its slice can only leak its
slice, can only be misled about its slice, and can be audited against its
slice.

## 4 · How memory rises

No world can keep everything at full detail forever, so Orreth does not
pretend to. Raw experience stays near the lives that produced it; each
tier summarizes before meaning climbs; and every summary is governed by a
**distortion contract** — a declaration of what must survive, what may
compress, and what loss is prohibited. A summary that violates its
contract is refused at save. Every summary keeps a signed chain back to
its sources, and the information lost is measured, not accidental.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart BT
    R0["Recent raw experience<br/>abundant · high fidelity · local"]
    D1["Field distillation<br/>dedup · summarize · preserve intolerables"]
    D2["Ecosystem distillation<br/>longer horizon · lower volume"]
    U["Universe memory<br/>all-time essential meaning"]
    SK["Crystallized skill<br/>procedural memory that earned reuse"]

    R0 -->|"distortion contract"| D1
    D1 -->|"signed derived_from chain"| D2
    D2 -->|"measured loss"| U
    U -->|"mentor craft + rubric"| SK
    SK -. "versioned Canon cascades down" .-> R0
`}</pre>

**The law it draws:** honest, governed degradation — never silent loss.
Learning is what makes safe forgetting possible: once a craft is proven
and crystallized as a skill, the details it was learned from may fade.

## 5 · What the machine believes

A signature proves who said a thing, when, and that it has not changed —
it never proves the thing is *true*. So every piece of knowledge carries
an **epistemic standing**, and standing outranks relevance in every
search: a perfectly matching claim from a discredited source still ranks
dead.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart LR
    V["verified / human-trusted"]
    C["corroborated"]
    U["untrusted / quarantined"]
    I["investigating"]
    R["recalled"]

    U -->|"independent receipts"| C
    C -->|"challenge / contradiction"| I
    I -->|"revalidated"| C
    I -->|"discredited lineage"| R
    V -->|"freshness or human challenge"| I

    R -. "ranks dead" .-> X["excluded unless explicitly asking for the dead"]
`}</pre>

An untrusted finding may still speak — dampened and labeled. Corroboration
counts independent voices, never repeated echoes. And when a source is
discredited, the recall walks its lineage: everything it taught the world,
and everything derived from that, is found and marked.

## 6 · The two books, one mind

The **Chronicle** is the world's memory of experience — what happened,
why, what it cost, who judged it. The **Canon** is the world's rulebook
and behavior — every policy, prompt, skill, and dial, versioned forever.
The loop between them is how the machine improves: Chronicle evidence
proposes a change, an independent seat grades it, a human gates the
consequence, and the new Canon governs the next Chronicle.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart TB
    HUMAN["HUMAN<br/>purpose, legitimacy, judgment"]
    CANON["CANON<br/>the rulebook — versioned behavior"]
    CHRONICLE["CHRONICLE<br/>the memory of experience"]
    IDENTITIES["LIVING IDENTITIES<br/>residents, workforce, services, minds"]
    WORLD["WORLD<br/>the environment perceived and changed"]
    GLASS["GLASS<br/>legibility without hidden authority"]

    HUMAN -->|"objective and consequence authority"| CANON
    CANON -->|"compiled law, apertures, gates, resources"| IDENTITIES
    IDENTITIES -->|"governed action"| WORLD
    WORLD -->|"observations and receipts"| CHRONICLE
    IDENTITIES -->|"thoughts, decisions, outcomes"| CHRONICLE
    CHRONICLE -->|"evidence and proposed change"| CANON
    CHRONICLE --> GLASS
    CANON --> GLASS
    GLASS -->|"understanding and judgment"| HUMAN
`}</pre>

**The law it draws:** a universe is a version of the Canon executing over
the state of the Chronicle, through governed identities, against a world,
under human purpose. No mechanism grades its own yardstick, and the
machine never renews its own ultimate purpose — that seat is the human's,
permanently.

## Where governance meets what you build

The kernel is deliberately purpose-blind: identity, one signed log, gates,
and meters — it does not know what any agent is *for*. Between the kernel
and your purposes lives the **resident firmware** — becky (identity), ada
(minds), charlotte (tools), the librarian (knowledge), vera (judgment),
governance (the floors). Everything you install — a
[capability](/build/first-capability/), a
[world of your own](/build/first-world/), an
[agent you bring](/build/bring-your-own-agent/) — is governed from its
first breath, because every join, thought, tool call, and record passes
through that firmware, and the kernel beneath has no other way to run it.

One razor keeps the layers honest: **what you can change in production is
purpose; what takes a release is firmware.** Your capability's prompts and
schedules stay editable through gates; the words the residents run on do
not.
