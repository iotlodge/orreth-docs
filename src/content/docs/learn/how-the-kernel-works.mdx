Six pictures explain most of kernel 0.1.0. Each starts with the plain version, then the diagram,
then the law it draws. Every diagram describes machinery that runs today; the
[what-works-today register](/learn/what-works-today/) stays the honest boundary.

## 1 · The road an ask takes

You type "echo, say the word HERON once". The kernel writes the ask and its fact on the ground in
one transaction; the relay publishes the fact; the dispatcher hands it to echo's bench; echo
serves it and its reply lands as a signed fact the same way. Under your ask a soft line — the
journey — says each step as it happens. Nothing ever travelled off the rails, so the whole road
can be read back later from the log.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
sequenceDiagram
    participant P as You (seated)
    participant K as the kernel
    participant G as the ground
    participant R as the rails
    participant B as echo (a body)
    P->>K: POST /ask {text, session}
    K->>G: ask row + outbox fact (ONE transaction)
    K-->>P: 201 {ids: [ask_…]}
    G-->>R: relay publishes orreth.ask.received.v1
    R->>B: the dispatcher hands it to echo's bench
    Note over B: journey: heard the ask, every word
    B->>K: the reply, signed by echo's key
    K->>G: reply + outbox fact (ONE transaction)
    G-->>R: orreth.reply.v1 (pointer + hash, never the words)
    R-->>P: the feed lights the panel; the chat shows the reply whole
`}</pre>

The laws: **transactional outbox, always** (a committed record cannot lack its event);
**pointer-only payloads** on the rails (the words stay on the ground); **every ask wears its
journey**; and **at-least-once with idempotent effects** — a fact is handed to exactly one body by
the inbox's footprint.

## 2 · How a body joins

A body — the crew's or yours — never simply connects. It knocks at the desk with its DID and its
public key, is challenged with the kernel's own nonce, proves it by signing, and then waits. The
crew is admitted at once on the manifest's spawn ticket; a self that was admitted here before is
admitted at once on its standing welcome; a stranger is **staged**, and its hold appears in the
chat with Cancel as the default. Only a governing seat's yes admits it. Then the body collects its
lease with the same key.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> pending : POST /join {did, name, role, public_key, template_hash, policy_hash}
    pending --> challenged : the desk's nonce, in the same breath
    challenged --> proved : POST /join/prove — a signature over {did, join_nonce}
    challenged --> denied : a forged proof (one face: "join refused")
    proved --> done : the crew's spawn ticket · a standing welcome
    proved --> staged : a stranger — join.admit held in the chat
    staged --> done : a governing seat clicks Yes
    staged --> denied : Cancel · fifteen minutes of silence
    done --> [*] : POST /join/lease — the lease, chained to the root, with the fuel clause
`}</pre>

The laws: **joining is a governed request**; **a keypair is a self** (the same key is admitted as
the same self, life after life); **scopes only narrow** (the lease opens the body's own words at
`/delta` and nothing more); **refusal wears one face**.

## 3 · How a person is seated

Every door reads who you are from a token the kernel signed, never from the page. You prove a code
from your authenticator; the kernel — this universe's own root — mints a capability token naming
you, your grants and your expiry, and the page carries it on every knock. On a ground nobody holds,
the first person to prove an authenticator becomes its owner.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart LR
    E["POST /enroll {person}<br/>→ the otpauth secret + a QR"]
    C["POST /enroll/confirm {person, code}<br/>the first code finishes enrolling"]
    S["POST /seat {person, code}<br/>→ the seat: a signed token,<br/>its wire form, your role"]
    D["every door:<br/>authorization: Bearer ‹wire›<br/>the feed: ?seat=‹wire›"]
    E --> C --> S --> D
    classDef human fill:#f2a91d,color:#101625,stroke:#ffd36a,stroke-width:2px;
    classDef k fill:#3536a4,color:#eef2ff,stroke:#8f91ff;
    class E,C,S human;
    class D k;
`}</pre>

The laws: the unseated wear one face (`401 {"error": "not seated"}`); a seated person lacking a
grant wears the proof's (`403`); the browser origin is closed; a knock ceiling stands at every door
per person. A seat lasts a day; "leave my seat" ends it early, recorded.

## 4 · How a red watch is cured

A watch is red while its condition holds. When one turns red, the kernel's standing Resiliency
intention wakes. The kernel reads a **dossier** off the ground first — the watch, who it names and
their state, the last acts on them, what happened last time — with no mind involved. It hands the
planner that dossier and the **lever catalogue** this door serves. The planner answers in the
catalogue: one lever and its reason. The kernel pulls the lever with its own hands, through the
same door a person would use, as a recorded hop in the intention's session. Then it reads the
watch again and says what happened.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart TB
    W["a watch turns RED<br/>orreth.watch.turned.v1"]
    O["an OBSERVATION lands<br/>under Resiliency"]
    DS["the kernel reads the DOSSIER<br/>off the ground — no mind"]
    PL["the planner answers IN the catalogue:<br/>“LEVER: body.restart name=echo — BECAUSE: …”"]
    R{"consequence?"}
    RT["routine — pulled at once"]
    CQ["consequential — held at the<br/>interlock for a person's click"]
    GR["grave — never pulled by the kernel"]
    RD["read the watch again after settles_s"]
    OUT["cured, with its cause · self-healed ·<br/>still red → once more, then the human with the dossier"]
    W --> O --> DS --> PL --> R
    R --> RT --> RD
    R --> CQ --> RD
    R --> GR
    RD --> OUT
    classDef k fill:#3536a4,color:#eef2ff,stroke:#8f91ff;
    classDef red fill:#7a1f2b,color:#fff0f0,stroke:#ff6b7a;
    classDef human fill:#f2a91d,color:#101625,stroke:#ffd36a,stroke-width:2px;
    class W red;
    class O,DS,PL,R,RT,RD,OUT k;
    class CQ,GR human;
`}</pre>

The laws: **the kernel never lowers the ladder because it is running on its own**; **every red is
answered and every green attributed** (a health check grades it); **the human can always stop** —
"stop the kernel's Resiliency intention" is a grave act, recorded, never a deletion.

## 5 · The four memories, one truth

A body has four memories and only one of them is the truth. **Working memory** is the mind
mid-thought (the checkpointer) and is never truth. **The Record** is every word, signed and kept
forever. **Understanding** and **the Digest** are projections over the Record — rebuildable
accelerations that cite their sources. A purge is governed and leaves hashes, never words.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
flowchart TB
    B["a body"]
    WM["WORKING MEMORY<br/>the mind mid-thought<br/>never truth"]
    REC[("THE RECORD<br/>every word · signed · forever<br/>corrections are siblings")]
    UND["UNDERSTANDING<br/>what it means"]
    DIG["THE DIGEST<br/>the short version,<br/>citing its sources"]
    P["you: verbatim recall<br/>by key · by ask · by timeframe"]
    B <--> WM
    B -->|"outcomes commit<br/>through governed doors"| REC
    REC -->|"after commit"| UND
    REC -->|"at session boundaries"| DIG
    UND --> B
    DIG --> B
    P <--> REC
    classDef k fill:#3536a4,color:#eef2ff,stroke:#8f91ff;
    classDef body fill:#087d86,color:#efffff,stroke:#55d8d7;
    classDef human fill:#f2a91d,color:#101625,stroke:#ffd36a,stroke-width:2px;
    class REC k;
    class B,WM,UND,DIG body;
    class P human;
`}</pre>

On kernel 0.1.0 the Record, recall by key and by window, sessions with their digests, the
profile, and the compliance export stand on both kernels. Understanding is lexical; an embedding
lane, the purge and the long-objective resume are owed — see
[what works today](/learn/what-works-today/).

## 6 · How two worlds speak

Every universe is a cell: its own kernel self, database and role, benches and topics, bodies and
keys, sealed from every other. Two cells that name each other speak over one door in messages
signed by their kernels' own selves, the peer's DID pinned on first sight. An ask that names a cell
routes home and is answered here; under partition it parks in plain words and resumes once.

<pre class="mermaid">{`
sequenceDiagram
    participant P as You (cell one)
    participant A as kernel A · u:one
    participant S as POST /seam (signed both ways)
    participant B as kernel B · u:two
    participant L as librarian@two
    P->>A: "librarian@two, what is the weather?"
    A->>S: the command, signed by A's self (nonce · 120 s window · epoch)
    S->>B: verified against the pinned DID
    B->>L: served by B's own librarian
    L-->>B: the reply
    B-->>S: signed by B's self
    S-->>A: landed once
    A-->>P: answered in cell two by librarian
    Note over A,B: B dark → the ask reads PARKED, "unreachable since"; B back → resumed once
`}</pre>

The laws: **cells = worlds** (physical isolation, a role that reaches no other database); **commands
route home**; **the stop rides the seam first** and lands within two seconds; **a re-homed universe
advances its epoch** and a stale epoch is fenced.
