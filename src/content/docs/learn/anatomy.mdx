## The kernel and its reference

**`orrethd`** is the kernel: one Rust program that is the door on port 4600, serves the one page
(the glass), keeps the ground's schema, runs the rails, seats the crew and holds the watches and
levers. It is the only thing in the world that signs as the world's root.

**The reference** is the same kernel written first in Python, on port 4601. It is not a fallback;
it is the yardstick. Every law is pinned by a conformance fixture both kernels must pass
unchanged — twenty-six fixture files today, 730 cases on the Rust side. When you read
"both kernels" in this book, that is what it means.

## The six organs

The panel draws six organs, always seated, because the kernel always carries them:

| Organ | What it is | Its doors |
|---|---|---|
| **DESK** | Who is seated and who may come in: the seat a person takes with a code from their authenticator, and the join desk every body walks through. | `/seat` · `/enroll` · `/join` |
| **GROUND** | The kernel's memory: one Postgres database, one schema with one writer ever, forty tables; every ask, reply, fact, lease, watch and meter line. | `/recall` · `/export` · `/fact/:id` · `/parked` |
| **GATEWAY** | The meter: LiteLLM, run and managed by the kernel. Every body has its own virtual key wearing its budget; every answer comes back with its cost. | `/minds` · `/minds/spend` |
| **SHELF** | The register of every service the kernel governs — tools, MCP servers, stores, sources and minds — on one lifecycle ladder. | `/services` |
| **MONITOR** | The operating state, live: the rails, the bodies, the asks, the watches, the health checks, the harness, and every lever. | `/monitor` · `/harness` · `/levers` · `/bodies` |
| **ANALYZER** | Everything born in the chat, grouped by origin — intentions, the objectives under them, in flight and done — read from the ground. | `/analyzer` · `/intentions` · `/markers` |

## The five rails

The rails are the kernel's embedded loops. Each is an organ of the running kernel, breathing on the
ground and visible on the panel as a segment that lights when a fact passes:

| Rail | What it does |
|---|---|
| **relay** | Publishes each fact from the outbox to RabbitMQ and Kafka after the transaction that made it commits. A committed record can never lack its event. |
| **dispatcher** | Hands each fact to the benches of the bodies it addresses. A fact that can never apply — bytes that were never an envelope — is **parked** with its evidence until a person advances past it. |
| **scheduler** | Ticks the standing duties: the librarian's hourly review, the kernel's harness run, any cadence you gave a body. Every occurrence is an ask. |
| **presence** | Keeps the bodies' leases while they serve; a lease that lapses reads dormant within seconds and the station goes dashed. |
| **intent** | Turns every intention: observes what it is interested in, asks its planner, files the objective, hears the runner, and re-plans. |

Underneath them are the three transports: the **ground** (Postgres) where truth lives, the
**invocation rail** (RabbitMQ) where work waits for the one body that claims it, and the **events
rail** (Kafka) where facts are published for anyone to replay. Every message on all three wears
the same envelope: canonical bytes, a content hash, the authority chain (who acted for whom) and
the marker (why). The glass listens to the **feed**, a stream of small notices; the browser never
touches a broker.

## The crew

The kernel seats ten bodies when it lights, each one its own Python process it spawns and
governs. They are declared in one manifest, `spine/crew.v0.json`, and every seat is a template
file plus, for the workspace agents, a binding.

**Residents** have a name and a persona. They are the ones who answer you:

- **librarian** — reads and recalls what this world knows; keeps your words in the Record; uses
  the weather, acquire, mark, seal and purge tools; reviews its day on the hour.
- **echo** — repeats your words back, with no mind, so you can see the road an ask takes.

**Firmware** bodies have a function and no persona. Same laws, different face:

- **planner** — writes the steps of an objective; answers a red watch with one lever from the
  catalogue.
- **critic** — marks improvements on what the crew did.
- **grader** — grades another body's work; it never grades its own yardstick.
- **mitl** — the Master Mind In The Loop: has read the architecture and the covenant from disk;
  says what an act touches before you decide.
- **toolkeeper** — tends the shelf: probes every service, versions a changed one, proposes a
  retire after three strikes, never retires alone.
- **stablekeeper** — tends the Stable: pings every mind, watches price drift and end-of-life,
  proposes a re-pin, a swap or a refill.
- **crew** and **monitor** — the workspace agents bound to the CREW and MONITOR pulls; the
  monitor's may propose a watch with the add-watch tool.

A body that dies comes back as the same self after a backoff. One that dies three times in five
minutes is **parked**, as a fact carrying its last words, and comes back only on a person's word.
A body refused at birth is never restarted. At dark, every body is stopped whole.

## The seat and the lease

Two things stand at the DESK, and they are the same shape: a capability token in the covenant's
form — subject, audience, grants, constraints, chain, signature — that can only narrow as it is
passed on.

A **seat** is a person's. You prove a six-digit code from your authenticator; the kernel signs a
token naming you, what you may do here (retrieve, write, and govern for the owner and masters) and
until when (24 hours). Every door reads who you are from the seat, never from the request. The
first person to prove an authenticator on a ground nobody holds is its **owner**.

A **lease** is a body's. It walks the join desk — challenged with the kernel's nonce, proves the
key behind its DID, is staged for a governing seat's yes (or admitted at once on the crew manifest
or its standing welcome) — and collects a token chained to the kernel's root with the **fuel
clause**: a dollar allowance and the window it renews in. The lease opens the body's own words at
the door and nothing more, for thirty days.

## The Stable and the shelf

The **Stable** is where minds stand: each is a stall with a pinned deal (provider, model, price)
and an entry in the gateway. Minds live wherever they live — Anthropic, OpenRouter, Ollama on your
machine, any OpenAI-compatible base — and a body is assigned one for all its work or for one class
of work (fast, standard, deep). A fresh world's Stable is empty until you add a mind or give the
gateway a provider key; a body without a mind says so honestly.

The **shelf** holds every service the kernel governs on one ladder: registered → versioned →
healthy or unhealthy → retired. Nine built-in tools are declared as data both kernels read; an MCP
server you add lists its tools onto the shelf as identities; a store or a source is a service
too. Retired is rest, recorded, never deletion.

## Cells and the seam

A universe's physical home is a **cell**: its own kernel self, its own database and role (a role
that reaches no other database), its own benches and topics, its own bodies and keys. Cells are
worlds. Two cells that name each other speak over one door, the **seam**, in messages signed by
their kernels' own selves; an ask that names a cell ("librarian@two, …") routes home and is
answered here. Under partition the ask parks in plain words and resumes once when the peer
returns. A re-homed universe advances its **epoch** and the old home refuses to serve.
